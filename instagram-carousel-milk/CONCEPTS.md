# The 12 competing concepts

Twelve creative directors each pitched under a different constraint. Four judges (growth strategist, comedy writer, art director, bored 26-year-old at 11pm) scored every pitch on hook, originality, humour or emotion, save/share value and visual feasibility, then named a top three. Rank is by top-three votes first, total score second.

## The pitches

**MILK RUN**  
A dead-serious heist briefing for stealing one glass of milk at 02:07 — every step oddly convenient, oddly suspicious — until the final slide reveals the guard was awake the whole time and set the job up for you.  
*Motif:* The wedge of fridge light. On slide 1 it is a hairline sliver cutting in from the right edge. On every slide it opens a few degrees wider, and the timestamp ticks forward one or two minutes. On the final slide the door is fully open, the wedge floods the whole kitchen, and that flood is what reveals the adult silhouette standing in the doorway. The light literally tells the plot.

**BEST BEFORE (a countdown, narrated by the date on your milk)**  
The inkjet expiry stamp on a milk carton confesses it's just a guess, then counts itself down from Day 7 to Day 0, teaching you the right way to drink milk on each day it has left.  
*Motif:* The dot-matrix date stamp, which ages slide by slide: on Day 7 the dots are crisp and perfectly aligned; on each subsequent slide the dots drift, one nudges out of the grid, a few fade to grey, one turns Sour Red, until Day 0 where the stamp is a smudge and a single dot has rolled off the carton entirely.

**IN THE UNLIKELY EVENT OF MILK**  
A laminated airline safety card, panel by panel, for the most dangerous beverage you will consume today: a glass of milk.  
*Motif:* The tilted white glass at exactly 45 degrees, with its dashed angle line and the little '45°' mono label, appears on every slide somewhere (huge on slide 1, tiny in a corner on the rest), the way an aircraft outline repeats across a real card. It becomes the brand mark of the series.

**The Rite of the Midnight Glass**  
A hushed, gilt-edged liturgy for the thing you were about to do anyway: stand in the fridge light at 11:47 PM, in socks, and drink one glass of milk correctly.  
*Motif:* The fridge-light wedge: an SVG polygon of Fridge Light entering from the right edge of the frame, slightly wider at the top than the bottom like an opening door, with a soft radial falloff into the navy. It is a 40px sliver on the hook and grows every slide (12, 38, 50, 62, 78, 90 percent) until it collapses to nothing on step VII and returns on the final slide as two slivers, one on each edge. A gold halo ring travels with it: faint above the glass, full above the head, and finally hanging alone in the dark.

**The Little Light Over the Stove**  
An eight-step instruction manual for drinking milk that is secretly the only recipe a grandfather ever left behind, told as a 3am kitchen where the last slide moves the light from his window to yours.  
*Motif:* The one lit window: a single small yellow rounded rectangle glowing on a dark field. It appears on slide 1 as his kitchen seen from outside, shrinks into the hood lamp over the stove for the middle slides, and returns on the last slide as one lit window in a whole dark apartment building: yours.

**The Tilt**  
Eight slides, one glass of milk, one number per slide: the degrees of tilt. Your swipe is the wrist, and the entire drama of drinking milk (the wait, the gulp, the stubborn last drop, the drop that hits your shirt) plays out as a physics diagram where the glass rotates and the milk, obeying gravity, stays perfectly level.  
*Motif:* The protractor. A thin Skim arc (radius ~430px) centered on the drinker's lower lip, ticks every 15 degrees, with one Tongue Pink tick marking the current angle. The glass rotates around that exact lip point every slide (CSS transform-origin at the pivot), and the milk surface inside it NEVER rotates: it stays dead horizontal on every slide, clipped by the rotated glass path. That level line is the quiet second joke that rewards a second look.

**GLASS RANKED**  
Milk-drinking treated as a competitive fighting game: an official tier list, frame data for your grip, a combo system, a skill tree, a banned-techniques list and a final boss, with your rank badge levelling up every time you swipe.  
*Motif:* A hexagonal rank badge fixed top-right on every slide that levels up as you swipe: UNRANKED, BRONZE, SILVER, GOLD, PLATINUM, DIAMOND, MASTER, GRANDMASTER. Each rank has its own fill colour and the badge animates nothing (static) but the label change slide-to-slide gives the reader a reason to keep swiping: you are ranking up by reading. A thin milk-cream progress bar under the badge fills one segment per slide.

**Nine Sips**  
The carousel is a glass of milk, every swipe is a sip, and your thumb is the mouth: the milk line drops one step per slide, the real "how to drink milk" rules ride along, and swiping back to slide 1 refills the glass.  
*Motif:* The meniscus, the milk's wavy surface line. It sits at 100% of the slide height on slide 1 and drops one step per slide (100, 88, 75, 62, 50, 38, 25, 8, 0 percent), and it always runs off the right edge mid-wave so the swipe reads as the continuation of the pour. Secondary motif: a drawn row of nine dots at the bottom that mirrors Instagram's real dots and keeps changing jobs (counter, thermometer, dunk timer, refill pointer).

**MILK: Owner's Manual (Rev. 9)**  
A deadpan appliance manual for the most familiar liquid on earth: nine numbered sections of real dairy science (judging temperature, light damage, fridge geometry, the official fault scorecard, casein-as-detergent, lactase genetics, hydration index), laid out like a Braun product booklet that takes milk more seriously than anyone ever has.  
*Motif:* THE UNIT: a cross-section tumbler (a 6px-stroke trapezoid, wider at top) with a dashed fill line and a tiny '▽ FILL' tick at the rim. It appears on every slide in a different state: stratified into layers, clamped to a thermometer, sitting in a fridge diagram, under a fluorescent tube, multiplied into 100 tiny units, turned into a gauge. Plus the Hazard Orange caution triangle (with the tumbler silhouette inside it instead of an exclamation mark) at the top-right of every header strip.

**IN THE EVENT OF MILK**  
A 1970s Pan Am 747 seat-pocket safety card, rebuilt panel by panel, that treats drinking a glass of milk with the same calm, laminated seriousness as a water landing.  
*Motif:* The seat pocket. Every slide's bottom 140px is a Clipper Blue seatback with a cream card edge peeking out of a pocket, and the card edge advances 1/9 further across the pocket each slide, like a progress bar disguised as upholstery. On the final slide the card is fully tucked in.

**Swipe to Drink**  
The whole carousel is the inside of one glass of milk: every swipe is a swallow, the milk line drops one etched graduation per slide, and when the glass is finally empty on slide 9 the base of the glass tells you to swipe back and refill.  
*Motif:* The meniscus. One horizontal milk line that drops exactly one graduation per slide, tracked by an etched ruler running down the left edge (ticks labeled 9 to 0 in mono, current level highlighted). The ruler is the carousel's progress bar and the milk level is the carousel's memory: from slide 7 on, ghost Frost lines remain on the glass wall at every previous level.

**THE MILK FILES (Case 0001)**  
A deranged investigator's conspiracy corkboard proves, exhibit by exhibit, that you have been drinking milk wrong your whole life, then the red strings all lead back to one unopened carton in his own fridge.  
*Motif:* The red string never breaks. On every slide at least one string enters from the left edge and exits off the right edge at the same vertical height, so when you swipe, the whole carousel reads as one continuous string that finally terminates in a single pushpin on the last slide. Every exhibit also carries a Manila evidence tag with a Ballpoint-blue case number: CASE 0001 / EXHIBIT A, B, C...

## What the judges said the winner had to nail

- BEST BEFORE wins because it is the only pitch where the concept, the facts, and the visual system all reinforce one idea: the number on the carton is a guess, and the stamp itself is the honest narrator. To land it, four things must be nailed. First, slide 1 has to read as an actual inkjet date at thumbnail size: the CSS circle grid must be crisp, high-contrast, and unmistakably the thing on a carton, with 'BEST BEFORE' legible before the joke, and the rogue dot visible, not hidden. Second, the aging motif must be obvious, not subtle: drifting, greying, blurring and the single Sour Red dot should be readable at a glance on every slide, because that decay is the swipe engine and the emotional through-line; if it is too quiet the deck becomes a listicle with a font. Third, keep the voice flat and mortal, no cow jokes, no wellness, and every fact verified and phrased to be repeated at a table (door shelf temperature, casein and capsaicin, sour milk as buttermilk); steal the 60°F 'you have never tasted milk' fact and the 4 ml empty-carton accusation to add two more screenshot slides. Fourth, handle the safety qualifier in character: a mono print-code line ('PASTEURIZED. KEPT COLD.') in the corner, never a disclaimer paragraph. Make Day 0 the quietest, emptiest slide in the deck, and make the closing fridge-door slide point at a specific person to send it to, not a generic save.
- The winner lives or dies on two things. First, absolute deadpan: not one exclamation mark, wink or cute adjective anywhere in the artwork; the card does not know it is funny, and the moment it does, the whole thing becomes a novelty listicle. Second, escalation through grammar, not repetition: seat map, do/do-not pair, order of service, brace position, water landing, night frame, evidence tag must each be a visibly different piece of the safety-card language, so slide 5 does not feel like slide 2 again. Cut every body line to the bone (the brief's word ceiling is real; several panels are over it) and keep milk the only pure white on the page so the eye finds it in 200 milliseconds. Slide 9 must work as a standalone screenshot: 4 ml, the ruler, the redacted suspect name, and the footer flipping to 'THIS CARD MAY BE REMOVED AND HANDED TO THE OFFENDER' is the share mechanic, so give it the biggest slash circle and the least clutter. Steal the seat-pocket progress bar from Pitch 8 and keep the pictogram people generic enough that no airline's lawyers recognise themselves.
- The winner lives or dies on the dot-matrix being a real typographic system, not a decoration: build proper 5x7 glyphs on a fixed grid with one dot size, so BEST BEFORE / 09 SEP reads as an inkjet stamp at thumbnail size on slide 1, and make the decay (drift, grey, blur, one Sour Red dot, one rogue dot rolling off) legible at 1080x1350 on a phone without a side-by-side. Slide 1 must work alone in the grid: the headline plus the signature is the whole pitch, so give it the entire frame and the one rogue dot, nothing else. Cut body copy hard; several slides run past the word ceiling and the stamp's voice is better in eight words than twenty. Keep the register flat and bureaucratic on every slide so the fridge-door sign-off can be tender without a wink. Fold the 'pasteurized, kept cold' qualifier into the mono print-code footer where it reads as label copy, not a disclaimer, and make the Day 0 'trust the nose' slide the quietest frame in the deck so the science claim feels earned rather than shouted.
- MILK RUN wins because it is the only pitch where the last slide is better than the first, and the share line is earned instead of asked for. The execution must nail three things. One: the voice stays cold and clipped for all eight steps, no soft adjective, no cute detail, no emoji, or the ending becomes a Hallmark card and the 26-year-old screenshots it to mock it. Two: the reveal has to add information the viewer did not already guess by slide 5. Everyone will clock 'you can reach it now' and 'bendy straw'; the payoff is that the guard was awake, does this every night, and the second clean glass is already on the counter for tomorrow. That second glass is the whole carousel, so it must be legible at phone size and not buried under copy. Three: the fridge-light wedge has to be readable on a dim phone in a dark room, since that is where this will be seen; a 15-degree hairline on slide 1 is a beautiful idea that becomes invisible at 30% brightness, so err wider and rely on the yellow-on-near-black contrast. Finally, honor the brief: smuggle real drinking instruction into the dossier annotations so it is actually 'how to drink milk' and not just a short film about a kid.

## Critique round

Four critics (scroll-stopper skeptic, comedy editor, mobile-legibility expert, demanding audience member) attacked the first draft; the rewrite addressed every must-fix. Their verdicts:

- Concept is a keeper and the last slide is genuinely screenshot-bait, but the script currently fails its own half-second test: slide 1 puts the only surprising words ("without waking the guard") in 46 px mono under a headline that is just the assignment topic, and stacks three text tiers plus header clutter on a hook. Momentum is strong through slide 4, then dies on 5-6 because slides 3, 4, 5, 6 tell the identical joke (something was conveniently prepared, don't ask) so the reveal is guessed by slide 5; slide 8 then prints JOB COMPLETE before the reveal, which is an exit sign. The real twist (the crew is a small child, the guard is a parent, 1,204 nights) is carried only by a silhouette-next-to-counter scale cue that most scrollers will not decode. Fix the hook hierarchy, break the repetition, replace JOB COMPLETE with a cliffhanger, and make the child reveal legible; do not touch the register, the palette or the ending.
- Ship the concept, halve the words. The heist-briefing-for-a-kid reversal is genuinely good and the second glass is a real payoff, not a shrug. But every step slide runs roughly double the 25-word ceiling because each one carries a body AND an INTEL note AND two or three map labels; the "Don't ask" refrain that should make "Never asked." land is inconsistent; slide 4 says its best line twice; and the closer stacks four punchlines around the one object the script itself calls "the whole carousel." This is a cutting job, not a rethink.
- Concept, voice and colour discipline are excellent and should not be touched. The production script, however, is over-written for a phone: every step slide from 2 onward carries 35-48 words on canvas against a 25-word cap, the body block as specified (one line per sentence, 46px, 5-7 sentences) physically collides with the illustration band on slides 6, 7 and 8, and the actual milk knowledge (the "how to") lives only in the 28px INTEL tier, which renders at roughly 10px on a phone and will not be read. Several stated layout beats also do not compute geometrically (the wedge never reaches MILK on slide 1 or the GO dot on slide 2; the slide 4 door rotation tilts rather than opens; the slide 9 counter lands at knee height, not hip). All of this is fixable with cuts and number changes; the concept survives intact.
- Save: yes. Send: yes, but only after a rewrite, and only because of two things the script currently buries. To the three questions. (1) Is there anything true in here or is it all bit? About 30 percent is real: don't keep milk in the door (warmest), bottom-back shelf is coldest, mouth-on-carton backwash sours it, and the one most people genuinely do not know: rinse a milk glass cold because hot water sets the protein. That last fact is the screenshot. The problem is the script files every true thing as INTEL at 28 px in Ghost (#3A4666 on #0B0F1A is roughly a 2:1 contrast ratio, about 10 pt on a phone). The only useful content is typeset as background texture; on a dim phone it does not exist. The bit is in Milk at 190 px, the truth is invisible. Invert that priority and the carousel stops being all bit. (2) Would I send it, with what message? Yes, to a parent or older sibling, and the message writes itself: "the towel is you." That is a real send, and it is why the piece can travel. But the send depends on the reader recognising the quiet helper in their own house, which the towel over the creaky board does in one image, and the itemised recap on slide 9 then over-explains. (3) Is the close earned? In concept, yes: SEND THIS TO WHOEVER PUT THE GLASS OUT is in-world and names a specific recipient, and JOBS LOGGED: 1,204. ARRESTS: 0. is the best seven words in the deck, the line that converts the bit into a feeling. In execution, no: that stat is in 26 px mono at the bottom of the busiest slide, under a headline (The guard was never asleep) that the reader guessed on slide 4 because the anomaly wink fires six times before the reveal, and next to a fridge-shaped save icon that is a generic CTA in a costume. Right now slide 9 asks three things (headline, figure, glass) and lists the answer key. Lead with the stat, cut the recap, drop the save glyph, and the close is earned. Overall: the engine (two clocks, growing wedge, palette flip), the towel, SLIPPERS SLAP, the level milk in the tilted carton, and 1,204/0 are the reasons to keep this. The word counts (slides 5 to 9 run 40 to 45 words against a 25 cap), the unreadable INTEL, and the over-telegraphed twist are the reasons it is not ready to post.
